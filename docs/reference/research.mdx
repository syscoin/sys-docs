Our 2021 scaling proposal started with a question about custody. What good is a balance you control if the only operator who will process your payment disappears? The essays below follow that question from payments to shared computing and, eventually, to the rights people can delegate to autonomous agents.

## Cryptography and proofs

- **Stateless hash-based signatures.** FIPS 205 defines SLH-DSA from SPHINCS+ and standardizes SHA-2 and SHAKE parameter sets. The candidate Core identity profile, `SLH-DSA-SHAKE-128s`, has a 32-byte public key and a 7,856-byte signature. Scheduled WOTS+ is a separate Syscoin protocol with a one-time-use signing rule. [NIST FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) · [Syscoin's ChainLock design](../architecture/post-quantum.mdx)
- **Limited-signature smart accounts.** The zkSYS precompile uses the separate `SLH-DSA-SHA2-128-24` profile from the NIST SP 800-230 initial public draft. That profile limits each signing key to 2²⁴ signatures. The account or signer must enforce its key-use limit because a stateless verifier cannot count signatures. [NIST SP 800-230 draft](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/230/ipd) · [Pinned verification and conformance notes](https://github.com/syscoin/zksync-os-server/blob/v0.24.4/contracts/README.md#slh-dsa-sha2-128-24-verifier-status)
- **Module-lattice signatures.** FIPS 204 defines ML-DSA. For a wire-size example, ML-DSA-44 uses a 1,312-byte public key and a 2,420-byte signature. Public-key size, signature size, signing policy, and cryptographic assumptions all matter when deciding what to deploy. [NIST FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final)
- **Hash functions.** RFC 7693 defines BLAKE2s, FIPS 202 defines SHAKE and SHA-3, and FIPS 180-4 defines SHA-256. Core PoDA accepts both BLAKE2s and legacy Ethereum Keccak-256. Exact algorithm, encoding, and domain matter when comparing or recomputing digests. [RFC 7693](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7693) · [NIST FIPS 202](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.FIPS.202) · [NIST FIPS 180-4](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/180-4/upd1/final) · [PoDA encoding](../architecture/data-availability.mdx#what-the-hash-commits-to)
- **Hash-based proof systems.** The original FRI paper from 2018 introduces a Reed–Solomon proximity test. Airbender uses DEEP-FRI, a refinement that queries the polynomial outside the original evaluation domain, as part of proving RISC-V execution. When zkSYS settles a wrapped proof, the assumptions of the deployed wrapper also apply to that batch. [Original FRI paper](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2018.14) · [DEEP-FRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12243) · [zkSYS proving and wrapping](../architecture/zksys.mdx#why-there-are-two-proof-stages)
- **What a program proof covers.** A zkVM proves execution of compiled code against specified inputs. A November 2026 preprint uses SP1 and Jolt to study how a compiler can change a program’s behavior while the proof still verifies the compiled execution. This is one reason the program and toolchain matter as well as the cryptography. [Tan, Chaliasos and Donaldson, *Proving the Wrong Program*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04701v1)
- **UTXO assets and Z-DAG.** The original Syscoin asset model uses explicit output dependencies. Z-DAG adds a pre-confirmation conflict signal for merchants. The Whiteblock report describes a controlled Core 4 workload. [Syscoin 4 whitepaper](https://syscoin.org/file/syscoin4-whitepaper.pdf) · [Z-DAG paper](https://syscoin.org/zdag_syscoin_whitepaper.pdf) · [Whiteblock report](https://syscoin.org/tps_whiteblock_syscoin_report.pdf) · [Current SPT flow](../tech/tokens.mdx)
- **Bitcoin interoperability research.** Our BitVM2 withdrawal proposal uses Bitcoin coinbase tags to detect forks. Witness encryption is a separate research direction that could use Bitcoin-anchored evidence as an input. [Design and assumptions](../architecture/bitcoin-bridge.mdx) · [Bitcoin bridge paper](https://syscoin.org/btc_bridge.pdf) · [BitVM2](https://bitvm.org/bitvm_bridge.pdf) · [PVUGC draft](https://github.com/syscoin/PVUGC/pull/1)

## Design essays by Jagdeep Sidhu

### From a computational court to authority over scarce resources

The 2021 proposal starts with a wallet holder. She needs control of her coins and a timely way to spend them. She can still hold the only signing key while an operator refuses to process her payment. Sidhu calls the shared base a *computational court*. Applications can do their own work if users have a common place to settle claims under rules they can check. Even after the court has settled her balance, the wallet holder still needs a way to get her next payment included. [Coordinated Financial Computing, 2021](https://jsidhu.medium.com/a-design-for-an-efficient-coordinated-financial-computing-platform-ab27e8a825a0) · [Rights when an operator stops cooperating](../intro/human-and-ai.mdx#if-the-operator-stops-cooperating)

Moving execution away from that court creates a data problem. What can a stranger recover and check when the computer that ran the payment disappears? The 2022 Von Neumann essay gives execution, temporary data and persistent records different jobs. Later that year the private Rollux testnet made the distinction visible. A user sent a transaction from Pali, the rollup published its batch, and a reader could retrieve the bytes from an indexer and check them against the recorded hash. The same question applies to zkSYS today. Gateway can carry compact references while readers retrieve the data from Core or an archive. Replaying execution also takes the agreed starting state and the block inputs. [Von Neumann architecture, 2022](https://jsidhu.medium.com/blockchain-as-a-coordinated-global-von-neumann-architecture-2876e3c7b0ea) · [Rollux demonstration](https://syscoin.org/news/revealing-the-method-in-the-madness) · [What survives the serving window](../architecture/data-availability.mdx#what-can-someone-verify-a-week-later)

Who keeps that shared infrastructure running? The 2024 Bitcoin modularity essay considers execution, data and the conditions for releasing BTC together. The April 2025 interview explains why Sidhu sees a role for BTC as reserve capital. Participating Bitcoin miners already reuse work on Syscoin and can earn SYS. A BTC bridge needs its own enforceable withdrawal rule. zkSYS takes the funding question one layer further. Its reference design ties reward eligibility to SYS stake or eligible Sentry collateral while letting applications choose their own execution rules. [Bitcoin modularity, 2024](https://jsidhu.medium.com/rollux-creating-a-new-layer-0-for-bitcoin-modularity-e2d737c99168) · [April interview at 20:18 and 23:07](#interviews-and-listening-guide) · [Bridge design](../architecture/bitcoin-bridge.mdx) · [zkSYS issuance](../architecture/zksys.mdx#sys-collateral-issuance-and-gas)

*Truth States* brings the question back to the person holding the right. Anyone may observe that a machine is free. Who may reserve it, for whom, and who may change that rule? As intelligence gets cheaper, many agents can propose good uses for the same scarce resource. A community can give an agent limited authority, publish the rule and let others check how it was applied. Distance makes the value of delegation especially clear. If Mars has exclusive authority over a resource, its users can act locally. Earth can verify the proof when it and the required data arrive. A transfer between the two still takes communication, and people still decide which allocation rules they consider fair. [Truth States, 2025](https://jsidhu.medium.com/truth-states-consciousness-and-decentralized-intelligence-d5415da47dc6) · [Verification across distance](../intro/human-and-ai.mdx#verification-across-distance)

The same division of work appears in Core’s PQ candidate. Each Core node verifies the hash-based finality signatures directly and can identify who signed. The zkSYS design puts application execution and proving above Core so new applications can do more work without making every Core node run them. A public Genesis payment has receipts on each leg of that journey. Its Gateway batch reached NEVM with a mock verifier. Production settlement would need an enforcing verifier, and the hardware and bandwidth an independent PQ node needs during a burst remain unmeasured in the published results. [Direct finality checks](../architecture/post-quantum.mdx) · [Public testnet trace](./zksys.mdx#trace-a-public-testnet-transaction) · [Operator measurements](../architecture/design-tradeoffs.mdx#keeping-up-at-the-tip-and-recovering-later)

### Earlier proposals for cross-chain data and Bitcoin collateral

We proposed a zkDA light client that could verify Syscoin data commitments on another chain. zkSYS addresses a narrower version of that problem today. Gateway receives the ordered hashes for one batch, and a reader can retrieve the bytes from Core and check them independently. The earlier Bitcoin Layer 0 work also explored difficulty-based stBTC. The later bridge paper gives a concrete withdrawal rule based on Syscoin references that miners publish on Bitcoin. ## Institutions, computation, and shared resources

- **Machine intelligence.** Alan Turing's 1950 paper frames machine intelligence through the imitation game and examines objections to machine thinking. It provides the historical context for Sidhu's later question about the institutions that coordinate powerful machine actors. [Computing Machinery and Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433) · [Turing's typescript, King's College archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/computing-machinery-and-intelligence)
- **Common-pool resources.** Elinor Ostrom compared successful and failed forms of self-governance of shared resources. Her prize lecture describes recurring features of durable arrangements, including clear membership and resource boundaries, participation by affected users, accountable monitoring, and local dispute resolution. Her later diagnostic work asks how rules should fit the actual participants, incentives, and resource. A cryptographic ledger can make parts of such a process auditable. Eligibility, dispute resolution, and the accuracy of physical measurements still depend on the institutions that supply and govern them. [Governing the Commons, Cambridge University Press excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811075/69782/excerpt/9781107569782_excerpt.pdf) · [Ostrom's 2009 prize lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/ostrom_lecture.pdf) · [A diagnostic approach for going beyond panaceas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2000497/)
